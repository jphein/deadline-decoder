01|When a letter from Social Security, the county, or a landlord says you have sixty days, or three days, which days does it mean? Deadline Decoder answers that in plain words, with the math shown.
02|It started with a real letter. The builder is a disability claimant whose Social Security claim was denied again this month. The letter says sixty days. It does not say that the count starts five days after the date on the letter, or that a deadline on a weekend moves to the next workday.
03|Not sure what kind of letter you have? Paste its words. The decoder recognizes the letter and finds the date on it. Nothing leaves your device.
04|Then it shows the real deadline. Fifty two days left. Ask for a hearing before a judge by Tuesday, November seventeenth. Every step of the count is shown, so a person, or a legal aid worker helping them, can check it.
05|Below the deadline: what to do, in order, and where to get free help. One tap adds the deadline and a one-week reminder to your phone's calendar. It can read everything aloud, make a copy for a helper, or print.
06|Eviction timelines are where mistakes cost the most. A California three day notice counts court days, not calendar days. Served the day before Thanksgiving, those three days run until Wednesday, December second, because the holiday, the day after, and the weekend don't count.
07|Court papers for an eviction now give ten court days to respond, since a 2025 change in the law. The decoder counts them and says plainly that doing nothing means losing by default.
08|Medi-Cal and CalFresh notices give ninety days to ask for a hearing, and the decoder explains how to keep benefits while you wait.
09|Every rule cites its source: the federal regulations, the California codes, and the court holiday calendar, which the tests check day by day. An optional button uses Claude to explain the whole letter in plain language, but the deadline never comes from the AI. It comes from the law, so it cannot be made up.
10|Deadline Decoder is a free, static web app. It works on any phone, keeps your letter on your device, and was built during LexHack for everyone who has ever stared at a letter wondering how much time they really have.
